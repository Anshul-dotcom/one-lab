TXN-003 — Next-month settlement
$890.00
Transaction recorded Mar 28 (late in month). Bank settled Apr 1 — outside the March close window. The books show $890 as receivable in March with no corresponding bank credit until April. At month-end the ledger overstates cash by $890.
Fix: use a "settlement expected" accrual entry at month-end, or extend the reconciliation window to T+2 across the month boundary and re-run the match on April 2.
TXN-005 — Rounding difference
$0.33
Platform charged $75.33; bank settled $75.00. Individually invisible but dangerous when summed across thousands of transactions — 10,000 such gaps = $3,300 lost per month. Root cause: bank truncates sub-cent amounts during batching rather than rounding.
Fix: enforce settlement-amount matching to 2 decimal places; flag any |delta| > $0.00. Escalate sub-$1.00 gaps to a suspense account for monthly write-off review.
TXN-007 — Duplicate entry
$4,500.00
TXN-006 and TXN-007 are same merchant (Zeta Pay), same amount ($4,500), same day, 8 minutes apart. Only one settlement (SET-005) arrived. TXN-007 has no settlement — it inflates platform revenue by $4,500 with no bank backing.
Fix: idempotency key on transaction creation (hash of merchant_id + amount + minute-bucket). Flag any same-merchant same-amount within 15 min window that lacks a unique settlement reference.
REF-001 — Orphan refund
-$567.00
Refund of $567 recorded Mar 22 with no original_txn_id. No matching original transaction found in the March ledger. Possible causes: original transaction was in a prior period, processed on a different platform, or the reference was lost in a data migration.
Fix: enforce a foreign key constraint — every refund must reference a valid txn_id. If the original is in a prior period, carry the txn_id forward and flag as "cross-period refund" rather than orphan.

Classic reconciliation problem. The core gap types you'll encounter are: (1) timing gaps — transaction recorded in one period, settled in the next. (2) amount variance — could be rounding truncation at the bank, FX conversion, or fee netting. (3) structural data issues — duplicate entries on one side, refunds with no originating charge. What's your data shape — do you have a txn_id/settlement_ref linkage, or are you fuzzy-matching on amount + date?

Assistant
Three big ones: (1) Multi-currency FX — $100 EUR doesn't settle as the same USD amount two days in a row. Your engine would flood with false rounding diffs. (2) Partial settlements — banks sometimes split a $10k ACH into two $5k wires. Your 1:1 matcher breaks; you need 1:N settlement grouping with sum equality. 1. Multi-currency FX rounding
This engine assumes USD throughout. In production, FX conversion introduces systematic rounding — a €100 charge may settle as $108.73 one day and $108.74 the next depending on the spot rate timestamp used by the acquiring bank. The engine would flood the ROUNDING_DIFF bucket with false positives unless currency code + agreed FX rate are stored with each transaction and applied consistently at match time.
2. Partial settlements and split batches
Banks occasionally split a single transaction across multiple batch runs — a $10,000 ACH may arrive as two $5,000 credits on consecutive days. The 1:1 match logic here flags both partials as unsettled rather than recognising them as a reconciled pair. A production engine needs 1:N and N:1 grouping with sum-equality checks, and a "partial settlement" status distinct from "unsettled".
3. Hardcoded timing windows are wrong for every real network
The 15-minute duplicate window and T+2 settlement assumption are synthetic. Visa settles T+1, Amex T+3, ACH next-business-day shifted by US federal holidays, SEPA T+1 by EU business calendar. Hardcoded windows cause false positives (flagging legitimate same-merchant daily charges) and false negatives (missing duplicates on slow networks). Production needs per-payment-method settlement SLA config and a holiday calendar.
